I would build the card around access, convenience and curation — things that feel valuable to the customer but cost Thexias relatively little to provide.

* colours

* budget range

Members see:

* new arrivals

₦15,000

A member can say:

Thexias searches and presents options.

Members get access to your Travel Edit service.

For example:

“I’m going to Cape Town for five days. Curate my wardrobe.”

You create the itinerary-based wardrobe.

You can then sell the actual clothing.

This is where the membership becomes a sales engine, rather than simply an expense.

Instead of giving 10% off the entire store, give something controlled:

₦10,000 birthday credit on orders above ₦75,000

That encourages an actual purchase.

Occasionally create:

THE PRIVATE EDIT

5–10 pieces available exclusively to members before public release.

Imagine you get 50 members.

Membership revenue

50 × ₦30,000 =

₦1,500,000

Now imagine your membership fulfilment costs average approximately ₦4,000 per person for:

* physical card
* premium envelope
* packaging
* delivery contribution/materials

50 × ₦4,000 = ₦200,000

That leaves approximately:

₦1,300,000

before your other operating costs and the value of your own time.

But the bigger opportunity isn’t the ₦1.3m.

It’s the additional shopping generated by the members.

Suppose only 30 of those 50 members spend an average of ₦150,000 with Thexias during the year:

30 × ₦150,000 = ₦4,500,000 in sales

If your average gross margin were 55%, that’s approximately:

₦2,475,000 gross profit

before fulfilment, payment fees, delivery, returns and other operating expenses.

So the membership effectively becomes a way of creating a high-intent customer community.
